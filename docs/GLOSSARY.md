# Glossary

Plain-language terms used in the UI, and the accounting/technical concept each one maps to. Per AGENTS.md §6: no accounting jargon in the default UI — say what a thing does, not what it's called in a textbook. Keep this updated as user-facing terms grow. Rows marked *(proposed)* await the maintainer's approval. German terms are given where suggested, since v1 ships in English and German (D-034).

| UI term | Means (technical/internal concept) |
|---|---|
| Account | A place where your money is: a checking account, a savings account, a credit card, or the cash in your wallet. German: *Konto*. Internal concept: an asset or liability account. Not the same thing as a Member (D-022). |
| Closed account | An account you no longer use, e.g. a card the bank replaced. It keeps its history but takes no new transactions and is hidden from the lists, until you open it again. It can be closed only when nothing is left in it. German: *Geschlossenes Konto*. Internal concept: an asset or liability account with the closed flag (D-081). |
| Category | What money was spent on or came from, e.g. Groceries, Rent, Salary. New users start with a built-in set (D-029). German: *Kategorie*. A category can sit inside another one, e.g. Groceries inside Food, to any depth. Internal concept: an income or expense account, with the category it sits inside as its parent (D-082). |
| Household book | Everything KassenSturz keeps about your money, in one place: accounts, categories, transactions, Rules. German: *Haushaltsbuch*. Internal concept: the book (one SQLite file with the extension `.ksz` in v1, D-033, D-037). |
| Rounded amount *(proposed)* | An amount shown without its decimal places because the user chose so in Settings → How amounts look, e.g. 54 € for 53,60 €. Only the display is rounded; pointing at it shows the exact amount (D-002, D-114). German: *gerundeter Betrag*. |
| Backup *(proposed)* | A complete copy of the household book that KassenSturz saves by itself before it updates the book to work with a new version, in its backup folder; the last 10 of each book are kept (D-033, D-080). German: *Sicherung*. |
| Open anyway *(proposed)* | What the user chooses when a book is marked as open on another computer, or still on this one after a crash: KassenSturz then takes the book over (D-038, D-080). Internal concept: taking over the book's lock file. German: *Trotzdem öffnen*. |
| Check against my bank | Typing what your bank says is in an account on a day, so KassenSturz can say whether it has the same and, if not, where the difference can come from. It never changes anything by itself (D-111, D-115). German: *Mit der Bank abgleichen*. Internal concept: `accounts::check_against_bank`, which holds the bank's balance against `Book::balance_on`. |
| Balance | How much money is in an account right now. For a debt it reads "You owe" (D-067). German: *Kontostand*. Internal concept: the sum of all parts booked to the account. |
| Starting balance | How much was in an account on the day you named when you added it, usually today (D-111). When your bank says something else for a day, "Check against my bank" offers to correct it; only you change it (D-116). German: *Anfangsbestand*. Internal concept: the opening balance, booked against an internal equity account the user never sees. |
| Stated balance | What you told KassenSturz your bank showed for an account on a day: when you added the account, or when you corrected its starting balance (D-111, D-117). The account's page says whether the account still holds it; if not, "Check against my bank" starts from it. Only you change it. German: *angegebener Kontostand*. Internal concept: `stated_balance` and `stated_on` on the account, used as the reference for the account's balance on that day. |
| Transaction *(proposed)* | One payment or deposit: money that went out of or came into one of your accounts, with whom, what for and when. German: *Buchung*. Internal concept: a double-entry transaction with its parts (D-017, D-083). |
| Register *(proposed)* | One account's list of transactions, newest first, with what was left in the account after each ("Then: 946,80 €"). Shown as "Show transactions" on the account and as the account's name in its title; the word "register" never appears in the UI. German: *Umsätze*. Internal concept: `Book::register` and the `AccountRegister` list model (D-118). |
| Not sorted yet | A transaction that has no category, e.g. one that came from the bank and no Rule matched. It shows up like that in the list, and the transaction's page sorts it into a category. German: *Noch nicht zugeordnet*. Internal concept: a part with no account (`Split::uncategorized`, D-088, D-118). |
| Left over *(proposed)* | On the overview, a month's money in minus its money out; when more went out, the line reads "Spent more than came in" instead (D-122). German: *Übrig* / *Mehr ausgegeben als reingekommen*. |
| Money in / Money out *(proposed)* | What came in through income categories and went out through spending categories. Transfers between your own accounts and starting balances count as neither. German: *Geld rein* / *Geld raus*. Internal concept: the totals of the income and expense accounts (D-021, D-089). |
| You owe | How a debt is shown, e.g. a credit card balance: "You owe €300" instead of "−€300" (D-067). Internal concept: the balance of a liability account. German: *Sie schulden*. |
| Rule | A user-defined condition-and-action pair that auto-categorizes transactions, modeled on email-client filters. Internal concept: `Rule` (conditions, actions, optional sub-Rules forming a tree). See D-014 in `docs/DECISIONS.md`. German: *Regel*. |
| Create a Rule from this transaction | A shortcut on a transaction's page: opens the Rule editor with the other party (or the name shown as From or To) and the category the transaction has already filled in. Nothing is saved until the user adds the Rule. German: *Aus dieser Buchung eine Regel erstellen*. |
| Rule suggestion | KassenSturz's offer to make a Rule after you sorted the same name into the same category by hand three times: "You have sorted Corner Shop into Food › Groceries by hand 3 times. Want KassenSturz to do it for you from now on?" Plain counting, no AI; "No, thanks" is remembered on this computer. German: *Regel-Vorschlag*. |
| Sub-Rule | A Rule nested under another. Only checked once its parent already matched; if it also matches, it refines/overrides the parent's result. German: *Unterregel*. |
| Pattern matching (advanced) | Regular expressions (regex), offered as an opt-in per Rule condition for users who want it — never required for basic use. See D-015. German: *Muster (fortgeschritten)*. |
| From / To | The other side of a transaction: shown as **From** on money you receive and **To** on money you pay. In the Rule editor the field is called **Other party**. Replaces the earlier term "Sender", which was wrong for payments. Internal concept: the payee/counterparty (name and IBAN). See D-034. German: *Von* / *An*; the Rule editor's "Other party" is *Zahlungspartner*. |
| Name shown *(proposed)* | The name a transaction shows as **From** or **To**. It starts as the other party's name, and a Rule can replace it with a clearer one, e.g. "Supermarket" for "REWE SAGT DANKE 1234". Replaces the earlier word "payee" in the UI. German: *Angezeigter Name*. Internal concept: the payee. |
| Undo / Redo | Take back the last change, or make a change you took back again (D-066). German: *Rückgängig* / *Wiederholen*. Internal concept: the change history. |
| Statement | The list of money that went into and out of one account over a period, as the bank sends it, e.g. as a file from online banking, with what was in the account at the start and the end. German: *Kontoauszug*. Internal concept: `kassensturz_import::Statement`, read from a file by an importer (D-091). |
| Already in your book | An entry of a statement that an import skipped, because the account has it already: the bank's own number for it is there, e.g. after importing the same file twice. The import summary counts them. German: *Schon im Haushaltsbuch*. Internal concept: a duplicate, found by the bank ID (D-030, D-092). |
| Maybe in your book already | An entry of a statement that looks like a transaction the account has, e.g. one you typed in before the statement came: the same amount, a few days apart. It isn't imported unless you say so. German: *Vielleicht schon im Haushaltsbuch*. Internal concept: a similar entry (D-092). |
| Import from my bank | Reading a file from online banking (CAMT.053, MT940, OFX or CSV) into one account, from that account's list (D-123). Every transaction is shown first; nothing changes until "Import", and Undo takes the whole import back. Internal concept: the import pipeline (D-092). German: *Von meiner Bank importieren*. |
| Keychain *(proposed)* | Where your computer keeps passwords for you, locked with your login: KWallet on KDE Plasma, Passwords and Keys on GNOME, the Credential Manager on Windows, the Keychain on macOS, or KeePassXC if you let it take over the desktop's Secret Service (D-150). KassenSturz saves a banking PIN there only when you choose so, and never anywhere else (D-057); you can remove it there yourself. German: *Passwortspeicher*, as the screens say it whichever program keeps it (KWallet's own name is the same word; GNOME's program is called *Schlüsselbund*, which the screens do not use on its own). Internal concept: the Secret Service, behind `kassensturz-platform`'s `Keychain` trait (D-147). |
| Twice in the file | An entry a statement file holds twice, by the bank's own number for it; KassenSturz counts it once (D-092). German: *Zweimal in der Datei*. |
| Not booked by your bank yet | An entry the bank lists but hasn't booked (a card payment on its way, a pending transfer); an import leaves it out, and it comes with a later statement (D-030). Internal concept: pending entry. German: *Von der Bank noch nicht gebucht*. |
| Exchange rate *(proposed)* | How much one unit of one currency costs in another, e.g. 1 EUR = 1.0856 USD, on a given day. Entered by hand in v1 (D-028). German: *Wechselkurs*. Internal concept: a row of the book's price table (`DatedRate`, D-079). |
| Replaced currency *(proposed)* | A currency that another one replaced at a fixed rate, such as the German mark, which the euro replaced at 1 EUR = 1.95583 DEM. Amounts in it, e.g. from an old book, come in converted into the new currency, and each Part keeps the amount it had, shown as "was 10.00 DEM". No account can be in one. For one KassenSturz doesn't know, such as the old Romanian leu, the user says which currency replaced it, at what rate and from which day. German: *Abgelöste Währung*. Internal concept: a withdrawn currency (`Withdrawn`), and a Part's original amount (`OriginalAmount`) with the fixed rate (D-077, D-104). |
| Part | One piece of a transaction broken down by what it was actually for (e.g. one €53.20 grocery charge split into Food, Household, Fast food Parts). Internal concept: `Split`. See D-017. A Part can have no category yet; a Part in another currency also shows its amount there, e.g. 100.00 USD next to €92.50 (D-043, D-083). German: *Teilbetrag*. |
| No category | What a Part shows until it has a category, e.g. right after an import, before a Rule or you pick one. Counts in no category's total. German: *Ohne Kategorie*. Internal concept: a split without an account (D-083). |
| Purpose | The text a payment came with, as the bank delivered it, e.g. an invoice number. German: *Verwendungszweck*. Internal concept: remittance information, kept unchanged with the Part the bank delivered (D-067, D-083). |
| Value date *(proposed)* | The date from which the money counts for interest, as the bank delivered it. Only shown in a transaction's details; the date shown everywhere else is the booking date. German: *Wertstellung*. Internal concept: `ImportedDetails::value_date` (D-030, D-083). |
| History | The list of changes made to the household book, newest first, so each can be undone and made again, also after closing the app. Kept for 30 days unless you choose otherwise. German: *Verlauf*. Internal concept: the change history (D-066, D-086). |
| Clean up history | Deletes the whole history for good, also from the book's file: nothing done so far can be undone anymore. German: *Verlauf bereinigen*. Internal concept: `Book::clean_up_history` (D-066). |
| Break down (a transaction) | The action of adding or editing a transaction's Parts, on the page "Break down this transaction" (D-119): what each part was for and how much, with one part's amount left empty for KassenSturz to work out. Available any time, not just at import. German: *Aufteilen*. |
| Scan a receipt | Post-1.0: photograph (Android companion app) or import an existing scan/photo of a receipt, so KassenSturz can suggest which transaction it belongs to and propose its Parts. Internal concept: `kassensturz-receipts` pipeline. See D-018. |
| Transfer | Moving money between two of your own accounts (e.g. Checking → Savings). Never counted as income or spending. Internal concept: a transaction whose splits are both real (asset/liability) accounts, no income/expense category. See D-021. KassenSturz suggests which two imported transactions are the two sides of one Transfer, and joins them into one when you confirm (D-087). Each account still shows its side with the date and payee its own bank gave (D-108). The question is "Money moved between your accounts?" with "Yes, join them" and "No, keep them apart" (D-120). German: *Umbuchung*. |
| Member | Post-1.0: a person using KassenSturz (e.g. each spouse in a household) — **not** the same thing as a financial Account; don't conflate the two. A Member sees only Accounts they own plus Accounts explicitly shared with them. See D-022. |
| AI memory | What the AI feature remembers about this book (its setup, and recurring patterns like "this payee is usually categorized as X") so it can be more useful without being re-explained every time. Never used to store actual balances or totals — those always come from code. Viewable, editable and deletable by the user; off entirely when AI is off. Comes after 1.0 (D-071). See D-023. |
| AI features *(proposed)* | An optional part of KassenSturz that lets you talk to an AI about your money and have it suggest changes. It is off until you turn it on in Settings; while it is off, nothing about it shows anywhere except that one switch, and nothing is sent anywhere (D-013). German: *KI-Funktionen*. Internal concept: the `ai` build feature and the AI-off switch (D-039). |
| AI provider *(proposed)* | The program or online service that answers your questions: one that runs on your own computer, such as LM Studio or Ollama, or an online service such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. You choose it and give KassenSturz its address and, for an online service, your key. German: *KI-Anbieter*. Internal concept: the provider behind the OpenAI-compatible adapter (D-035). |
| Your key *(proposed)* | The secret code an online AI service gave you so it knows the requests are yours. KassenSturz keeps it like a bank PIN, in your computer's keychain, and never in your book or in a file. A program on your own computer usually needs none. German: *Ihr Schlüssel*. Internal concept: the provider API key (D-169). |
| Your own AI tool *(proposed)* | An AI program you use outside KassenSturz, such as a desktop chat app or a code editor, that can read your book through `kassensturz-mcp` once AI features are on. It can only read, unless the user lets it prepare changes, which the user then confirms in the KassenSturz window (D-050, D-194, D-217, D-219). German: *Ihr eigenes KI-Programm*. Internal concept: an MCP client using KassenSturz's stdio MCP server; "MCP" stays out of the default UI and is named only in the setup notes. |
| Let my AI tools prepare changes *(proposed)* | The switch that lets the user's own AI tools prepare changes, which arrive in the chat sidebar and change nothing until the user confirms them (D-219). German: *Meine KI-Programme dürfen Änderungen vorbereiten*. Internal concept: the MCP write tools, `[AI] McpWriteTools`; the request folder `mcp-requests` stays out of the default UI. |
| What is sent *(proposed)* | The plain statement KassenSturz shows before you use an AI provider for the first time: which parts of your money data go to it, and whether it is on this computer or another one. You can look at the exact message and remove parts before it is sent, and you are asked again if the provider or the statement changes. German: *Was gesendet wird*. Internal concept: consent, preview and redaction (D-035, D-168). |
| Another computer *(proposed)* | Any AI that is not running on the computer you are sitting at, including one at home on your own network. Your data leaves this computer, so KassenSturz asks you first, just as for an online service (D-035, D-175). German: *Ein anderer Computer*. |
| Chat *(proposed)* | The panel where you ask the AI about your money, for example "Where did most of my money go last month?". Every figure in an answer comes from KassenSturz's own calculations, never from the AI's guess, and a number that did not is marked. Answers are labelled as coming from an AI. German: *Chat*. Internal concept: the chat panel (D-047, D-052); the marking is the number check (D-188). |
| What the AI looked at *(proposed)* | The list under a chat answer of what the AI asked KassenSturz for and what was sent to it, so you can see why it said what it said. German: *Was die KI angesehen hat*. Internal concept: the tool-call log (D-047). |
| Look at the request *(proposed)* | The window that shows, before a question goes to an AI on another computer, exactly what will be sent. You can leave a word or phrase out and see the message again, then send it or not send it. German: *Anfrage ansehen*. Internal concept: the per-request review (D-035, D-210). |
| Suggested changes *(proposed)* | A list of changes the AI prepared, for example a new category, a new Rule, or categories for a number of transactions. You see them first; nothing changes until you read them and press "Confirm and make these changes", and "Undo" takes them back like any other change; the history calls them "Applied changes the AI prepared" while AI is on and "Applied suggested changes" while it is off (D-198, D-200, D-213). German: *Vorgeschlagene Änderungen*. Internal concept: a change set (D-047, D-032). |
| One-press questions *(proposed)* | The three buttons in the chat that ask a fixed question for you: suggest categories for transactions that have none, explain this month, prepare Rules. The answer is labelled as the AI's, its numbers are checked, and what it prepares is a list of suggested changes you confirm (D-222, D-223). German: *Fragen auf Knopfdruck*. Internal concept: a built-in feature (`Feature`). |
| Problem log *(proposed)* | An optional log, off until you turn it on, that notes what KassenSturz did and which errors happened, without any amounts, names, texts or passwords. While it is on, and only while the AI features are on too, the AI may read it to help find out what went wrong. German: *Problemprotokoll*. Internal concept: the debug log (D-173). |
| Report a problem *(proposed)* | The AI can write a short report of a problem from a fixed form (version, error codes, screen, steps). You read the exact text first; if you agree, your own web browser opens GitHub's issue page with it filled in, and you send it yourself. KassenSturz sends nothing itself. German: *Problem melden*. Internal concept: the issue draft (D-173). |
| Test version *(proposed)* | What a build is called before the first release: it works but may have bugs. KassenSturz tells the AI so, so it says "this might be a bug" instead of "you did it wrong" (D-174). German: *Testversion*. Internal concept: pre-release build (D-152). |
| Whole book (file) | A file with everything in the book (accounts, categories, transactions, Rules, exchange rates) that you save with "Save the whole book" and can start a new book from, for example as a backup or on another computer. Internal concept: the complete export (D-100). The history of changes isn't in it. German: *Das ganze Haushaltsbuch*. |
| Spreadsheet file | A file of your transactions that Excel, LibreOffice Calc and similar programs open, one line per transaction and account. Internal concept: the CSV export (D-099). German: *Tabellendatei*. |
| Bank login *(proposed)* | Your access to one bank for fetching transactions: the login name your bank gave you and your PIN, set up once per bank in KassenSturz. KassenSturz only reads from your bank; it never sends money (D-053). German: *Bankzugang* (one word). Internal concept: a login in the bank backend (`kassensturz_bank::Login`), kept in AqBanking's own folder, never in the household book (D-139, D-145). |
| Get new transactions | Fetching what your bank has booked since the last time, into an account, the same way a file from your bank is imported: you see what is new, decide about look-alikes, and KassenSturz checks its balance against the bank's (D-029, D-030). German: *Neue Buchungen abrufen* (the verb is always *abrufen*, never *abholen*). Internal concept: `Bank::fetch`, `kassensturz_bank::statement`, then the import pipeline (D-145). |
| Password storage *(proposed)* | The setting that says where KassenSturz keeps a PIN you chose to save: your system's keychain (the default), or, one step away under "Show advanced choices", a KeePassXC database (on Linux, D-164) or nowhere at all. Changing it removes the PINs saved in the old place; you type each again once (D-163). German: *Passwortspeicher*, the group in Settings; its default choice is *Passwortspeicher des Systems*. Internal concept: `SecretStorage` in `kassensturz-platform`. |
| Remember PIN *(proposed)* | Your choice each time your bank asks for the PIN: type it every time (the default), keep it until you close KassenSturz, or save it in your system's keychain. A PIN your bank calls wrong is forgotten at once (D-057, D-140). German: *PIN merken*. Internal concept: `PinMode` and `PinSession` (D-145). |
| Linked account *(proposed)* | An account that KassenSturz fills from one of your bank accounts, named by its IBAN or, for a card, by its number at the bank; the book also remembers the day it was last fetched. The link holds no login name or PIN, so it travels safely with your book; on another computer you only set up the bank login again (Q-086, D-148). German: *verknüpftes Konto*. Internal concept: a row of `bank_links` for the account. |
| Unknown bank *(proposed)* | KassenSturz knows the IBAN's bank code but no bank with it, so it can't fill in the bank's details. You can type the bank's FinTS address yourself (D-151), and the project adds the bank to its list (`docs/BANK_LIST.md`). German: *Unbekannte Bank*. Internal concept: `Setup::UnknownBank`; `Setup::NoServer` and `Setup::UnknownLoginKind` are its siblings for a bank without a known server or login type (D-145, D-146). |
| TAN *(proposed)* | A one-time number your bank asks for to confirm a step, e.g. the first connection; it comes from your TAN app, a text message or a TAN generator. KassenSturz sends it to the bank once and never keeps it. Like the PIN, it shows as dots while you type, until you press the eye button (D-162). A TAN the bank shows as a picture (flicker code, photoTAN, QR code) can't be shown yet. German: *TAN*. Internal concept: `Tan` and `BankDialogs::tan` (D-151). |
| Approve in your bank's app *(proposed)* | Instead of a TAN, some banks ask you to confirm in their app on your phone; KassenSturz shows the bank's question with the bank's own buttons and waits until you press one. German: *Freigabe in der App*. Internal concept: a Gwenhywfar message box, `BankDialogs::question` (D-151). Technical term kept out of the UI: "decoupled". |
| FinTS address *(proposed)* | The web address of your bank's server for programs like KassenSturz, starting with `https://`. It normally comes from the bank lists; you type it only where they lack it. German: *FinTS-Adresse*. Internal concept: the server address `adduser` gets (D-149, D-151). |
