# Notes for the user guide

What the user sees and does, step by step, and the messages they can meet. Each M3 step adds a few lines here, so the handbook can be written from them later.

## Your book (M3.1)

- **At the first start** KassenSturz makes your household book by itself, in its own folder, and opens it every time after that. You don't have to choose a file or know where it is.
- **Settings → Your book** shows the book's name and folder. "Open another book…" and "Start a new book…" are there for people who want more than one book, e.g. to try something out; most households need only one. KassenSturz then opens the book you used last.
- **Backups:** before KassenSturz updates a book to work with a new version, it saves a copy in the backup folder shown in Settings, and keeps the last 10 copies of each book.
- **"This book is open on Laptop":** a book is open in one place at a time. Close it on the other computer first, then choose "Try again". If KassenSturz isn't running there anymore, e.g. after a crash, "Open anyway" opens it here.
- **"This book is still marked as open on this computer":** KassenSturz may be open in another window, or it didn't close properly last time. If it isn't open anymore, choose "Open anyway".
- **"KassenSturz can't find your book":** the book you used last was moved, renamed, or is on a drive that isn't connected. Connect the drive and choose "Try again", or open the book where it is now. KassenSturz never makes a new, empty book in its place on its own.
- **Network drives:** a book on a network drive (e.g. a NAS share) opens with a warning, since network drives can damage a book. Keep it on your computer; a sync folder such as Nextcloud's is fine.
- **In the Flatpak** KassenSturz can't open books outside its own folder yet, and says so (Q-071).

## How amounts look (M3.2)

- **Settings → How amounts look** starts with your regional settings, e.g. 1.234,56 € in Germany. You can change the decimal separator, the thousands separator, whether the currency symbol comes before or after the amount, whether amounts below zero have a minus sign or parentheses, and whether decimal places are shown. The example at the top shows the result straight away.
- **Choosing a separator the other one uses** swaps them: with a decimal point in Germany, thousands are separated by commas, so an amount can't be misread.
- **Rounded amounts:** without decimal places, 53,60 € shows as 54 €. Only the display is rounded: KassenSturz adds up the exact amounts first, so rounded figures may not add up exactly on screen. Point at an amount to see it exactly.
- **Typing amounts** follows the same separators, and you can always type all the decimal places.
- These settings belong to this computer, not to the book: someone else opening the same book sees amounts their way.

## Accounts and categories (M3.3)

- **Accounts** (side menu) shows your accounts side by side with what is in each, in the order you added them. A credit card reads "You owe 300 €". From two accounts on, "All together" shows what you have minus what you owe. If you have accounts in more than one currency, each currency has its own group and its own total: KassenSturz never adds euros and dollars together.
- **Add account:** choose what kind it is, give it a name, say how much is in it and on which day. The day is today unless you change it, and the amount is what the account held at the end of that day. Leave the amount empty if there is nothing in it. "This account is not in EUR" (or whichever currency you use) lets you choose another one, by its code.
- **Open an account** by clicking its card: rename it, check it against your bank, or close it. You can close an account when nothing is left in it, so no money is hidden; it keeps its history and you can show closed accounts and open one again.
- **Check against my bank:** type what your bank says is in the account on a day. KassenSturz says whether it agrees. If not, it shows the difference exactly and where it can come from: a transaction that may be in twice or the wrong way round, a transaction that may be missing, transactions or a starting balance dated after that day, and what the starting balance would have to be for both to agree. It changes nothing by itself; it offers to correct the starting balance, see "The first start".
- **Messages:** "This day hasn't come yet" (type today or an earlier day); "This account still has 100,00 € in it. Move the money to another account first, then close it."
- **Categories** (side menu) lists where money comes from, then what it is spent on, with categories inside categories indented, e.g. Groceries inside Food. Click one to rename it or move it, with everything inside it, into another category or to the top level. "Add category" makes a new one, on its own or inside another. Categories can't be deleted yet.

## The first start (M3.4)

- **Welcome:** the first screen says what KassenSturz does and asks which currency you use. Your region's currency is already chosen, so most people only confirm; you can add an account in another currency later. "Add your first account" takes you on.
- **Your first account:** choose what kind it is, give it a name, and say how much is in it and on which day, as on "Add account". Take the balance from your bank's statement or app, so KassenSturz starts from what you really have.
- **Your first transaction:** add something you paid or received, for example your last shopping trip, or "Skip for now". Then the overview appears and the side menu is there.
- **Categories to begin with:** KassenSturz gives you a set in your language: where money comes from (Salary, Pension, Benefits, Interest, Gifts received, Money back, Other income) and what it goes on (Home, Food, Getting around, Health, Insurance, Clothes, Children, Pets, Free time, Education, Gifts and donations, Fees and charges, Other spending), several with categories inside, e.g. Groceries and Eating out inside Food. They are yours: rename, move or add to them under Categories.
- **Correcting the starting balance:** when "Check against my bank" finds a difference, it also offers "Set the starting balance to 800,00 €" (or "Add a starting balance of …" if the account has none yet). That is for a difference from before you started using KassenSturz, e.g. after you imported older transactions. Click it and the account holds on that day what your bank says; your other transactions stay as they are, and Undo takes it back. KassenSturz never changes the starting balance on its own.
- **Messages:** "The starting balance is dated after this day, so it can't be corrected from here. Check a day on or after the starting balance."
- **What your bank said (M3.4a):** the account's page remembers the balance you took from your bank and the day: "On 1 Oct 2026 your bank said 1.000,00 €, and KassenSturz agrees." If you later add a transaction dated before that day, the account no longer holds what the bank said, and the page says so: "On 1 Oct 2026 your bank said 1.000,00 €, but KassenSturz now has 975,00 € for that day. A transaction dated before that day may have changed it. Check against your bank to put it right." Its button opens "Check against my bank" with that balance and day already filled in, compared, and the correction offered. Correcting the starting balance from a check also updates what the page remembers to the balance and day you checked. Accounts added without a balance, and accounts from before this, remember nothing, and the page shows nothing about it.

## Transactions (M3.5)

- **Show transactions** on an account's page, or a click on an account card on the overview, lists everything that went into and out of that account, newest first. Each line says who the money came from ("From: Example Employer Ltd.") or went to ("To: Corner Grocery"), the date and the category, the amount, and what was in the account after it ("Then: 946,80 €"; on a credit card "Then you owed: 389,90 €"). The balance is at the bottom.
- **Sorted by** in the toolbar changes the order: date, amount, from or to, or category, and turns it around ("Oldest first", "Smallest first", "Z to A"). The "Then" amounts don't change with the order: they always count in date order.
- **Add transaction** on the list adds to that account; on the overview you choose the account first if you have more than one. Say what happened (paid or received), how much, from or to whom, what it was for and when. The amount is typed the way your settings show amounts, e.g. 53,20 in Germany. "Not sorted yet" leaves the category open; you can sort it later.
- **Click a transaction** to change any of this or remove it. Removing takes it out of the list and the balance; Undo brings it back.
- **What can't be changed on this page:** the starting balance ("To change it, check the account against your bank"; the button takes you there) and money moved between two of your accounts, which can be removed and typed again as its two sides (see Moving money between your accounts). A transaction broken down into several parts opens its own page (see Parts). A transaction that came from your bank keeps its amount as the bank sent it; the rest can be changed.
- **Messages:** "Type an amount."; "This isn't an amount. Type it like this: 1.234,56"; "Type an amount above zero. Whether you paid or received it, you choose above."; "This isn't a date. Type it like this: 19.10.2026"; "This transaction came from your bank or a file, so its amount stays as it was delivered. You can change the rest."; "This transaction isn't in the book anymore. It may have just been removed."

## Parts (M3.6)

- **Break down this transaction…** at the bottom of a transaction's page opens the page where you say what each part of it was for: for example one grocery bill as 40 € Groceries, 8 € Household goods and 5,20 € Eating out. A transaction that already has parts opens this page directly from the list. **Add a part** adds a line; the button at the end of a line removes it.
- **One part's amount can be left empty.** KassenSturz works it out from the total and the other parts and shows it in the empty field ("Part 3 gets what is left: 5,20"). When you open a transaction, its last part is shown that way, so you can add parts and type their amounts, and the last one shrinks by itself.
- **It has to add up.** Below the parts the page says "The parts add up to 53,20 €, the whole transaction." or what is still to be sorted or too much. If every amount is typed and they don't add up, saving is refused and the message says by how much. A part can have a minus sign, e.g. a deposit paid back on the same bill.
- **A part in another currency:** a category that counts in another currency is listed with it, e.g. "Travel (USD)". Such a part gets a second field, "In USD:", for what it was in that currency, e.g. from the receipt, and the page shows the rate the two amounts make ("1 EUR = 1,0811 USD"). If your book has an exchange rate for that day, the amount is filled in from it ("your rate of 1 Oct 2026") and you can overwrite it. Where the book has none and nothing is typed, saving asks for it.
- **What stays as it is:** money moved to another of your accounts in the same transaction ("Moved to Savings: 80,00 €") and the total of a transaction that came from your bank. The parts around them can be changed.
- **Messages:** "Add at least one part, so the transaction says what it was for."; "Only one part can be left without an amount; KassenSturz works that one out. Type the amounts of the others."; "The parts add up to 53,00 €, but the transaction is 53,20 €. Sort the remaining 0,20 € into a part, or leave one part's amount empty and KassenSturz works it out."; "The parts add up to 54,00 €, but the transaction is only 53,20 €. Take 0,80 € out of the parts, or leave one part's amount empty and KassenSturz works it out."; "Part 1: This part counts in USD, and your book has no exchange rate for it yet. Type what the part was in USD, e.g. from the receipt."; "Part 1: The two amounts of this part point different ways. Give both the same sign."; "Part 2: This isn't an amount. Type it like this: 1.234,56"; "This transaction can't be changed on this page. Go back to the list and open it again." (a transaction booked to the same account twice, or in another currency than the account).

## Moving money between your accounts (M3.7)

- **Type each side in its own account.** When you move 100 € from checking to savings, add "I paid money 100 €" in checking and "I received money 100 €" in savings, both with "Not sorted yet" as the category. Your bank's transactions look the same, so this is also what an import adds.
- **KassenSturz asks.** After you save the second one it asks "Money moved between your accounts?": "100,00 € left Checking account on 5 Oct 2026." and "100,00 € arrived in Savings on 6 Oct 2026.", with the name each account shows. The two have to be the same amount in the same currency, at most three business days apart (Monday to Friday), in different accounts, and neither may have a category.
- **Yes, join them** makes them one move of your own money: it no longer counts as spending and income. Both accounts keep their balance and the date and name they showed, and Undo takes it apart again. **No, keep them apart** changes nothing; KassenSturz doesn't remember the answer, and asks again only if one of the two is saved again while it still has no category.
- **Several at once:** the questions come one at a time ("Question 1 of 2"). Closing the window leaves the rest apart.
- **Message:** "This choice isn't there anymore. Choose again." appears in the window if one of the two was changed while the question was open; choose "No, keep them apart" to go on.

## Undo, redo and the history (M3.8)

- **Undo and Redo** are in the side menu and on Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z, and they say what they act on: "Undo: Removed a transaction". With nothing to undo they are grayed out. Undo takes back the newest change first, an older one only after the newer ones, and it works after you close and open the book again.
- **A notice after every change** says what was done, with an "Undo" button: "Removed a transaction". After an undo it says "Undone: Removed a transaction" with a "Redo" button, and after a redo "Made again: …" with "Undo".
- **Telling similar changes apart:** the side menu's name is short, but Settings › Show history says what the next Undo and Redo touch: "2 transactions added · Checking account · 3 Oct 2026 to 9 Oct 2026", and for an imported statement the file's name.
- **Show history** (Settings) lists the newest 100 changes, newest first, with the day and time; the ones taken back say "taken back; Redo can make it again". A new change ends what Redo could make again.
- **Keep the history for** (Settings): only while the book is open, 1 week, 30 days (to begin with), 3 months, 1 year, or as long as the book exists. Choosing less asks first, e.g. "12 changes are older than this and will be deleted for good. You won't be able to undo them or make them again." Your accounts and transactions are not affected.
- **Clean up history** (Settings) asks first: "This deletes the whole history for good, so nothing you did so far can be undone or made again. … Copies of the book that KassenSturz saved before an update (backups) keep their history; cleaning up does not touch them."
- **Undoing a long way back:** after undoing everything, the book is empty again and KassenSturz shows the first steps; they work as on the first day.
- **Messages:** "The book doesn't match its history anymore, probably because another program changed it. Nothing was changed. To go on with Undo, open Settings and use "Clean up history"."


## The overview (M3.9)

- **Overview** (side menu, and the screen after the first steps) shows your accounts side by side with what is in each today, then one month: "Money in", "Money out" and "Left over: 300,00 €" (or "Spent more than came in: 50,00 €"), then "Where your money went" and "Where your money came from" by category, largest first. Money you haven't sorted into a category yet is listed as "Not sorted yet".
- **Another month:** the arrows next to the month's name go back as far as your book has transactions, and forward to this month or further if you entered something for a later month. "Back to this month" returns to today's month, as does coming back from another screen.
- **Money moved between your accounts**, e.g. to savings, is neither money in nor money out, so the month says "Money you moved between your accounts (once this month) isn't counted as money in or out." A fee on such a move counts as money out. Starting balances aren't counted either.
- **Several currencies:** each currency has its own accounts, totals and categories; nothing is added up across currencies. A category that counts in another currency, e.g. Travel in dollars, is listed with what you paid from your account.
- **Latest transactions** lists the newest five across all your accounts; click one to change it, or click an account's card for everything in that account.
- **Messages:** "Nothing came in or went out this month yet. Add what you paid or received, and it shows up here."; "No transactions yet. Add what you paid or received, and it shows up here."

## Importing a file from your bank (M3.10)

- **Import from my bank** is on an account's list of transactions. Download a file from your online banking first: CAMT.053 (.xml), MT940 (.sta), OFX (.ofx) or CSV (.csv or .txt). KassenSturz recognizes the first three by their content; a file it doesn't know gets the message "KassenSturz doesn't recognize this file. It reads statements as CAMT.053 (.xml), MT940 (.sta), OFX (.ofx) or CSV (.csv, .txt) files; download one of these from your online banking." A KMyMoney file isn't a statement: "This file holds a whole book … A new book can be started from it instead."
- **CSV files** open "How to read this file": your bank's name (the choices are kept under it and offered for the bank's next file), the text encoding, what is between the columns, lines to skip, whether the first line names the columns, the decimal mark and the date order, then which column holds what, picked from the first line's names. "The first lines, read this way" shows the file as your choices read it, so a wrong separator is visible at once. The amount can be one column with a minus sign, two columns (money out, money in) or an amount with a column saying out or in (e.g. S and H). "Read the file" goes on to the preview; a wrong choice says what to check, e.g. "\"31.12.2026\" should be a date, but KassenSturz can't read it in the order of day, month and year chosen."
- **A file for several accounts** asks "Which statement is for this account?" and lists each with its account number, transactions and closing balance.
- **The preview** ("Import into Checking account") says what will happen before anything changes: how many transactions are new, how many your Rules sort, how many "look like transactions already in your book" (maybe you typed them in before the statement came; tick "Import it anyway, it is a different transaction" to import one, the lookalike from your book is shown beneath), how many are already in your book and skipped, and how many your bank hasn't booked yet. Every transaction is listed with its date, name, purpose and amount. "Your bank's balances" compares what the bank says before and after the transactions with what KassenSturz has for those days: "KassenSturz agrees." or "KassenSturz has 1.005,00 € for that day, 990,00 € more."
- **Import** adds the new transactions as one change; "Don't import" leaves everything as it was. The summary ("Imported into Checking account") repeats the counts, and when a balance agrees, KassenSturz remembers it as what your bank said (the account's page tells you if that stops being true). When one differs, nothing is changed about it; "Check against my bank" opens with the bank's figure and day filled in and explains where the difference can come from. A CSV file without balances gets the hint to use "Check against my bank" on the account's page.
- **Money moved between your accounts:** after the import, KassenSturz asks "Money moved between your accounts?" for each new transaction that looks like a move to or from another of your accounts, one at a time, as after a transaction you typed.
- **Undo** takes the whole import back, the remembered bank balance included; the history names the file.


## Rules (M3.11)

- **Rules** (side menu) sort new transactions for you, for example everything from your supermarket into Groceries. The list shows each Rule with what it does ("Sorts into Food › Groceries", "Shows the name … as From or To"); a Rule inside another is indented. **The first Rule that fits decides**, so each Rule has arrows to move it up or down. Without Rules the page says "You have no Rules yet".
- **Add a Rule** has a name, "When a transaction fits" with one or more conditions, and "Then": into a category, move it to one of your accounts (money moved between your own accounts, not spending), and optionally show another name as From or To. With several conditions you choose "All of these conditions" or "At least one of these conditions". A Rule only sorts transactions that have no category yet; what you sorted yourself is never changed.
- **A condition** looks at the other party (the name your bank sent), the other party's IBAN, the purpose, the name shown as From or To, the amount, the date, the currency or an account. Text can "contain", "be exactly", "start with" or "end with" what you type, ignoring capital letters unless you tick "Capital letters must match". **Use a pattern (advanced)** lets an expert type a regular expression instead; the screen explains it in one sentence and nothing else mentions it. For the amount you pick "paid" or "received" and then "Any amount", "Exactly" or "Between", and type amounts without a minus sign, in either order for a range.
- **Change or remove a Rule** by clicking it. "Also sort again what this Rule sorted before" shows how many transactions would be sorted differently (and how many can't be, for example without an exchange rate) before you save; without it only new transactions follow the changed Rule. Transactions you changed yourself are left alone either way. "Add a Rule inside this one…" makes a sub-Rule, which is only checked when the outer Rule fits and can set a more specific category or name. Removing a Rule removes the Rules inside it; what it sorted stays sorted. Undo brings it back.
- **Create a Rule from this transaction…** is on the page of a transaction you open from the list. It fills in the other party (or the name shown as From or To) and the category, you adjust it, and nothing is saved until you press "Add Rule".
- **The suggestion:** after you sorted the same name into the same category by hand three times, a notice at the top of the account's list and of the Rules page says "You have sorted Corner Shop into Food › Groceries by hand 3 times. Want KassenSturz to do it for you from now on?" with "Create a Rule" and "No, thanks". It isn't shown when a Rule already decides for that name, and "No, thanks" is remembered on this computer. It is plain counting and works with AI off.
- **Messages** name the condition: "Condition 2: This isn't an amount. Type it like this: 1.234,56"; "Condition 1 of Supermarket has no text to look for. Type some, or remove the condition."; "Give the Rule a name."; "Supermarket already has this name here. Choose another one, so you can tell them apart."

## Saving a copy and starting a book from a file (M3.12)

- **Save a copy of my data** (Settings › Your data) has two parts. "For a spreadsheet" saves the transactions of the accounts you tick (all of them to start with) as a file Excel or LibreOffice Calc opens; "More choices for the file" sets the separator, decimal mark, header line and a mark that helps Excel show letters like ä, and the usual choices fit your region. "The whole book" saves everything in one file you can start a new book from, e.g. as a backup; the history of changes isn't in it.
- Both ask where to save and **never overwrite a file**: "There is a file called … already. KassenSturz never overwrites a file, so choose another name, or move the old file away first. Nothing was saved." Afterwards the page says "Saved in transactions.csv" and how many transactions and accounts are in it.
- **Start a book from a file** (Settings › Your data, the first screen's "I have a KMyMoney book or a saved KassenSturz book", or the button on the notice when you import a KMyMoney book into an account) takes a KMyMoney book (.kmy or SQLite) or a whole-book file. The page shows what the file holds and where it goes: "Your book is still empty, so the file fills it", or "KassenSturz makes a new book called … and opens it. Your current book isn't changed; you can open it again in Settings with Open another book…". KassenSturz never changes the KMyMoney file.
- **Currencies KassenSturz doesn't know** (old currencies, e.g. the Romanian leu before 2005) ask "Replaced by", "How many ROL was one of the new currency worth?" and "From which day?". "Use what the file suggests" fills the fields from the file's own rates for you to check; nothing is used without your click. Every converted transaction keeps its original amount.
- **Your book is ready** lists what came in; "Worth a look" (transactions without a category as in the file, ones that didn't add up, names that got a number, closed accounts that still hold money stay open, converted currencies, pairs that may be money moved between your accounts); and "Left out", exactly what KassenSturz can't hold yet (budgets, schedules, tags, notes…).
- **Messages:** "This is a KassenSturz book already. To use it, open it in Settings with "Open another book…"."; "This is a statement from a bank, not a book. To bring it in, open an account's list of transactions and choose "Import from my bank"."; "Tick at least one account."; "This isn't a rate for ROL. Type it like this: 1.234,5678".

## Plain language and German (M3.13)

- **The language follows the system.** On a computer set to German, every screen, button and message is in German, addressing the user as "Sie"; on any other, it is English. There is no language setting in the app yet. A word with no German translation is shown in English, which is why a check (`tools/check-translations.sh`) fails when one is missing.
- **One word for each thing.** A line of a bank statement or an item in the list is always a **transaction** (German *Buchung*), the people and shops money goes to or comes from are the **other party** (*Zahlungspartner*), and the name a transaction shows is the **name shown as From or To** (*Angezeigter Name*). The word "payee" and the word "entry" for a transaction are gone from the screens. Moving money between two of your own accounts is a **Transfer** (*Umbuchung*), and the pieces of a transaction are its **parts** (*Teilbeträge*). `docs/GLOSSARY.md` lists the German words.
- **Technical words stay only where the user must choose with them:** CSV, IBAN, CAMT.053, MT940 and OFX in the file settings and the message about a file KassenSturz can't read, and "Text encoding" with "Tell from the file" as the first choice. "Use a pattern (advanced)" explains a regular expression in one sentence.
- **Messages:** unchanged in meaning; they now exist in German too, e.g. "Das ist kein Betrag. Geben Sie ihn so ein: 1.234,56" and "Es gibt schon eine Datei namens „bank.csv“. KassenSturz überschreibt nie eine Datei, wählen Sie also einen anderen Namen oder verschieben Sie zuerst die alte Datei. Es wurde nichts gespeichert."
- **The app's description** (software centres) no longer says the app saves nothing.
